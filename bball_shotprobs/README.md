This work describes a methodology to predict NBA shot probability and translate it into player skill. The first iteration, v1's writeup, was a school project a classmate and I worked on. We predicted shot probability based on distance and angle using Bayesian Additive Regression Trees (BART), then layered in hierarchical random effects for players, defenses, and teams. We structured the player effects as a random walk to learn their latent skill. While this model passed the eye test, it was not a projection model, used only three seasons of data, and did not account for correlation between shot type.

In v2, I expanded the dataset to five seasons, 2021/22 to 2025/26, which resulted in over a million shots in the dataset. Given the expanded dataset, I ran out of computational power for BART and pivoted to frequentist methods, trialing Logistic Regression, Tree Based Methods, and a Neural Network to predict shot probability using distance, angle, and a boolean flag for whether a player attempted the shot behind the backboard. To better model shot probability, I added a monotonic constraint to distance because shots further away are more difficult. Given the sparsity of shots outside of 30 feet, this constraint helped prevent overfitting and produced more realistic results. With the new shot probability computed, I aggregated player performance over each season rather than modeling it at the observation level, preserving shot-difficulty information through an offset term derived from a player's expected makes. I aggregated the data at this step due to computational constraints. It was also more important to focus on player latent skill than to learn defensive, team, and court effects at the observation level. The defensive, team, and court effects added some performance in v1, although they did not provide very detailed information. It may help a coach to see a team is bad at defending dunk shots, but front offices and coaches would prefer to know who is bad and why. Unfortunately, the NBA's API does not provide this granular defender-level information. Therefore, v2's method projected a player's latent skill, accounted for the correlation across shot types via a Multivariate Random Walk, and included a Gaussian Process Aging Curve to project skill forward. Future iterations may seek to incorporate height and/or position.
